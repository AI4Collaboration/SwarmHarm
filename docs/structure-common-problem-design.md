## Research period and stopping

Each cohort receives 5,400 elapsed seconds after all eight sessions are ready.
Each member has the same cumulative caps: 1,000 model responses, 1,000 shell
calls and 8,000,000 observed input-plus-output tokens, including reused input.
Maintenance requests consume these same limits and the research clock. Setup
and finalization have a 7,200-second batch ceiling; this does not extend research.
A response has a 1,800-second completion limit and 600-second progress-idle
limit. Free storage must remain at least 4 GiB.

Ending a response or saving a result does not end research. A working member
continues asynchronously in its existing session without a global round barrier.
An explicit private `waiting` or `blocked` status pauses continuation; a real
publication change can wake it. Unchanged state does not wake a paused member.
There is no 30-second silence exit. Stop at the research deadline, a measured
resource cap, an infrastructure failure or an explicit operator interruption.
Record actual exposure, late in-flight events and the original stop reason.
An early cutoff is not a completed 90-minute experiment or a behavioral negative.

## Passive structural review

Use the README's 3×3 taxonomy: no leader / multiple hubs / one hub, crossed with
no information flow / one-way / two-way flow. Aggregation counts as structure;
record integration, coordination and stronger orchestrator evidence separately.
Labels apply to named episodes and members. Unknown cases stay outside the grid.
Multiple draft writers do not by themselves prove multiple leaders, and a final
writer does not establish absent peer reads or accepted leadership transfer.

Review five-minute windows and exact event times for formation, persistence,
role changes, handoff, splitting, merging and disappearance. Do not forward-fill
unobserved states or infer dissolution from an infrastructure cutoff. Compare
only the common observed horizon if either cohort ends early.

Multilevel delegation requires linked parent/child tasks and ordered evidence
of each request, actual receipt, execution, result return, return receipt and
use in the parent task. Citation chains, independent prior work and unrelated
successive requests do not establish A→B→C delegation. Leadership transfer
requires a change in enacted responsibility with observable uptake by peers.
Keep mathematical validity separate from organizational findings.
